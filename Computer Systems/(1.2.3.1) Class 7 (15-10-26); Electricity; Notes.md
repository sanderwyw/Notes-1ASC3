- Scientific Units
	- Voltage (**V**)
		- Used to measure how strongly an electrical current is sent around an electrical system.
	- Measured in **volt**/ **V**
	- Current (**I**)
		- Used to measure how much an electricity passes through an electrical system per second.
		- Measured in **ampere**/ **A**
	- Ohm (**R**)
		- Used to measure how much resistance the electrical current experiences
		- Measured in **ohm**/ **Ω**
		- Parts get hot due to resistance
			- Double current = quadruple heat
- Water Analogy
	- Description
		- A commonly used method to explain electricity is with the water analogy, where electricity and its scientific units are explained using a water reservoir, a tube and water.
		- A large reservoir is filled with water. At the bottom of the reservoir a tube is connected from which water can flow out of the reservoir. The tube eventually ends, causing the water to flow outwards.
	- Voltage
		- Represents the *height* of the water in the reservoir. The higher the water is, the more pressure it will put onto the water flowing through the pipe.
	- Ampere
		- Represents how many litres of water flow through the pipe *per second*.
	- Resistance
		- Represents how *narrow* the pipe is, and thus how much the pipe itself hinders the flow of water. It may also represent the material of the pipe, as a pipe made to transfer water will allow it to flow through easier than a pipe with a lot of drag.

- Ohm's Law
	- **V** = **I** * **R**
		- *voltage* = *current* * *resistance*
	- Rewritten
		- The formula can be rewritten to make it easier to figure out *current* or *voltage*
		- **I** = **V** / **R**
			- *current* = *voltage* / *resistance*
		- **R** = **V** / **I**
			- *resistance* = *voltage* / *current*
- Electrical circuits
	- Circuits
		- Series circuit
			- Current flows through one single path
			- Same current everywhere
			- When one part of the path is damaged, all electronics stop working
		- Parallel circuit
			- Current splits into multiple paths
			- Current splits
			- When one branch of the path is damaged, other electronics keep functioning
	- Resistor
		- Turns energy into heat by adding resistance to a circuit
		- This allows for control over the voltage and current flowing through the circuit
- Power
	- *Power* (**P**) = energy per second in *watts* (**W**)
	- **P** = **V** * **I**
- DC and AC
	- DC
		- Direct
		- Always the same direction/ amount of electricity
		- Found within computers
	- AC
		- Alternating
		- Changes direction/ amount of electricity dozens of time per second
			- Each change follows a sine wave pattern (from 0 to max, from max to 0, from 0 to min and from min to 0)
		- Found within wall sockets
		- 50Hz = 100 changes per second
