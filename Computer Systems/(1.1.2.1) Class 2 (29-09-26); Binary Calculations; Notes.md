- Addition of binary numbers
	- Addition in Number Systems
		- When you add two numbers that are in the *same slot* and the sum *exceeds* the *limit* of the number system, a *carry* appears, meaning that you **add 1** to the number in the slot to the *left* and that for the current slot you write down the outcome of the *result* minus the *limit* of the number system.
			- Example 4**7**+2**5** (decimal)
				- 7 + 5 = **12** (exceeds *number limit*)
				- Calculation current slot = 12 - 10 (*number limit*) = **2**
				- Calculation slot to the *left* = 4 + 2 + **1**
	- Bits, Bytes and Number Ranges
		- Bit
			- *Smallest* Unit of Data Storage
			- **2** values only (0 or 1)
		- Byte
			- A group of **8** bits
			- 2^8 = 256 
			- **256** different *values*
		- Nibble
			- A group of **4** bits
			- Half a byte
			- 2^4 = 16
			- **16** different *values*
		- Number Ranges
			- kilo = 1000 = 10^3
				- Not a power of 2 = computer *doesn't* like it (doesn't understand *decimal*)
			- 2^10 = 1024
				- *Is* a power of 2 = computer *does* like it (only understands *binary*)
				- Closest power of 2 to 1000
		- Prefix Families
			- SI
				- International System of Units
				- Also used for scientific analysis and calculations
				- Measurements
					- Kilo
						- kB
						- 10^3
						- 1 000
					- Mega
						- MB
						- 10^6
						- 1 000 000
					- Giga
						- GB
						- 10^9
						- 1 000 000 000
					- Tera
						- TB
						- 10^12
						- 1 000 000 000 000
			- IEC
				- International Electrotechnical Commission
				- Measurements
					- Kibi
						- KiB
						- 2^10
						- 1 024
					- Mebi
						- MiB
						- 2^20
						- 1 048 576
					- Gibi
						- GiB
						- 2^30
						- 1 073 741 824
					- Tebi
						- TiB
						- 2^40
						- 1 099 511 627 776
			- Computer storage is usually sold in *SI measurements*
				- This causes the number on the package to be *bigger* than what *actually* shows up in your computer
					- Computers show you your storage in *IEC*
					- One *IEC unit* is worth *more* than one *SI unit* 
						- 1 pound =~ 1.17 euro
						- 1 euro =~ 0.85 euro
		- Storing Numbers with Fixed Number of Bits
			- Fixed numbers
				-  A computer gets a *fixed* number of bits to work with
				- Typically 8, 16, 32 or 64 (powers of 2) (computers love binary)
				- Range = 
					- 0 up to 2^n -1
					- 2^n possible values
			-  Overflow
				- A result being *larger* than the range results in an *overflow*
				- Extra bit that would be *added* in the *front* is *lost*
				- Value rolls over to **0**
				- Example (decimal)
					- Limit of *three* digits
					- 999 + 1
					- = **1**000 (limit of three digits, 4th digit is scrapped)
					- = 000
				- Example (binary)
					- Limit of *eight* digits
					- 1111 1111 + 1
					- = **1** 0000 0000 (limit of eight digits, 9th digit is scrapped)
					- = 0000 0000
