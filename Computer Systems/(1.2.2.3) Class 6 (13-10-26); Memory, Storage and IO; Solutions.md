	- A4) (think) A laptop with 8GB of RAM becomes very slow with 60 browser tabs open, while the processor is only 20% busy. Explain with the memory hierarchy.
		- While the processor is really fast, it is limited by the small amount of RAM. The RAM will be filled up by the 60 tabs, forcing the OS to make a pagefile which is drastically slower. Instead of quickly fetching data from the RAM, the processor will have to fetch data from the SSD or hard drive, which is many times slower. **

	- B3) A 3.5" platter has a radius of 4.75cm. How fast (km/h) does its outer edge move at 7200RPM
		- Circumference of circle = 2 * r * pi
		- 2 * 3.14 * 4,75cm = 29,85cm
		- 29,85cm = 0.0002985km
		- 7200RPM = 432000RPH
		- 0.0002985km * 432000RPH = 128.952 km/h
	- B4) Why is the start of a hard disk faster than the end?
		- The disc fits more sectors on the outer edges than on the inner inner areas.
	- B5) Your friend defragments her SSD every week "to keep it fast". Is this a good idea?
		- Because each piece of data on an SSD can be accessed equally as fast, this does absolutely nothing and your friend is just wasting her time.

	- C3. You have 2 × 4 TB and 2 × 8 TB. How much space do you get in RAID 5? Can you do better with two RAID 1 pairs?  
		- RAID 5 needs at least 3 disks, so the 8TB disks can only be used up to 4TB
			- 4 * 4TB = 16TB
			- 16TB - 4TB (one disk) for parity = 12TB of fillable space/ 16TB total space 
		- RAID 1
			- 4TB + 8TB = 12TB/ 24TB total space
		- Both take up the same space and are equally safe, but RAID 1 is simpler
	- C4. Rebuilding a 16 TB disk runs at 160 MB/s. How long does it take? Why is RAID 6 safer here than RAID 5?
		- 16 000 000 / 160 = 100 000 seconds or ~1667 minutes or ~27 hours
		- With RAID 5, when another disk dies while a disk is being rebuilt, it results in total data loss. This stems from the fact that RAID 5 only allows one disk to die, not two. RAID 6 allows for two disks to die, meaning that while a disk is being rebuilt another disk can die without data being lost. If two disks were to die however, this would still result in data loss (although the chance for this to happen is inconsequentially small)
