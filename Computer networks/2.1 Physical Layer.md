- Physical Layer Characteristics
	- Physical Connection
		- Networks need a physical connection to a local network
		- Connection can be wired or wireless
		- A *NIC* connects a device to a network
			- Physical card that is part of a device (like GPU)
			- Usually part of motherboard
		- Not al physical connections offer the same level of performance
	- Physical Layer Characteristics
		- Bandwidth
			- Properties
				- The capacity at which a medium can carry data
				- *Digital bandwidth* measures the amount of data that can flow from one place to another in a given amount of time (measured in bits/ second)
				- **Physical media properties**, **current technologies** and **physics** play a role in the amount of *bandwidth*
			- Bandwidth terminology
				- Units of bandwidth
					- Bits (1 bit)
					- Kilobits (1.000 bits, 10^3)
					- Megabits (1.000.000 bits, 10^6)
					- Gigabits (1.000.000.000 bits, 10^9)
					- Terabits (1.000.000.000.000 bits, 10^12)
				- Latency
					- Amount of **time** for data to travel from one point to another
				- Throughput
					- Amount of **bits** that are transferred across the media over a given period of time
				- Goodput
					- Amount of **usable bits** transferred across the media over a given period of time
	- Cable types
		- Copper cabling
			- Unshielded twisted-pair (UTP) cable
				- **Most common** networking media
				- Ends in *RJ-45* connectors
				- Interconnects **hosts** with **intermediary network devices**
				- Characteristics
					- **Outer jacket** protects wires from **physical** damage
					- **Twisted pairs** protect the signal from **interference**
					- **Color-coded plastic insulation** which **isolates** the electricity flowing through from each other and allows for easy **identification**
			- Shielded twisted-pair (STP) cable
				- Better **noise protection** than UTP
				- More **expensive** than UTP
				- **Harder to install** than UTP
				- Ends in *RJ-45* connectors
				- Interconnects **hosts** with **intermediary network devices**
				- Characteristics
						- **Outer jacket** protects wires from **physical** damage
						- **Braided** or **foil** shield provides **EMI/RFI** protection
						- **Foil** shield for each pair of wires provides additional **EMI/RFI** protection
						- **Color-coded plastic insulation** which **isolates** the electricity flowing through from each other and allows for easy **identification**
			- Coaxial cable
				- Characteristics
						- **Outer cable jacket** to prevent **minor physical** damage
						- **Woven copper braid** or **metallic foil** that acts as **shield** for inner conductor and as **second wire** in circuit
						- Layer of **flexible plastic insulation** around main conductor
						- Main **copper conductor** which is used to **transmit electronic signals**
				- Many different connector types
						- BNC
						- N type
						- F type
				- Uses
						- Attaching antennas to wireless devices
						- Cable internet installations
		- UTP cabling
			- Properties
				- **Four pairs** of color-coded copper wires **twisted together**
				- Encased in flexible **plastic sheath**
				- No shielding
			- UTP relies on the following properties to limit crosstalk
				- Each wire in a pair of wires uses opposite polarity. This causes the magnetic fields to cancel each other out
				- Variation in twists per foot in each wire
			- Standards
				- Standards for UTP established by the TIA/EIA
					- Cable types
					- Cable lengths
					- Connectors
					- Cable termination
					- Testing methods
				- Standards for copper cables established by the IEEE
					- Category 3 (four cables)
					- Category 5 and 5e (four sets of twisted pairs of cables)
					- Category 6 (four sets of twisted pairs of cables encased individually)
				- RJ-45 connector
					- imagine average ethernet cable
		- Fiber-Optic Cabling
			- Types of fiber media
				- Single-mode fiber
					- Very small core
					- Expensive lasers
					- Long distance applications
				- Multimode fiber
					- Larger core
					- Less expensive LEDs which transmit at different angles
					- Up to 10 Gbps over 550 meters
					- Increased loss of signal strength
						- Maximum cable distance 550 meters
				- Connectors
					- Straight-tip (ST) connectors
					- Lucent connector (LC) simplex connectors
					- Subscriber connector (SC) connectors
					- Duplex multimode LC connectors
				- Cords
					- SC-SC MM patch cord (orange)
					- LC-LC SM patch cord (yellow)
					- ST-LC MM patch cord (orange)
					- ST-SC SM patch cord (yellow)
					- Yellow jackets are for single-mode fiber cables and orange/ aqua cables are for multimode fiber cables
			- Uses
				- Optical fiber is used as the backbone cabling for high-traffic connections between data distribution facilities and for the interconnection of buildings in multi-building setups
				- Higher bandwidth than UTP
				- Long distance (100.000 meters compared to 100 meters)
				- Immunity to EMI and RFI
				- Immunity to electrical hazards
				- High cost
				- High installation skills
				- High safety precautions
		- Wireless media
			- Wireless LAN requires the following devices
				- Wireless access point (AP)
					- Gathers wireless signals from users and connects them to a copper-based network infrastructure
				- Wireless NIC adapters
					- Provides wireless communications capability to network hosts
			- There are many different WLAN standards. Make sure they are compatible when you buy them
			- WLANs need security policies and processes to protect them from unauthorized access and damage
